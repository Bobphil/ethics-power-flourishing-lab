# ETHICS-PFLAB-2 — Power and Flourishing Laboratory Reconciliation

## Status

**FROZEN PRE-INTERFACE RECONCILIATION SPECIFICATION**

This build reconciles the existing `ETHICS-PFLAB-INTEGRATION-1` Lab with the final Ethics Pressbook (`ETHICS-PB-PROD-3`), the existing SUPER handoff contract, and later FSP governance improvements.

It does **not** replace the deployed Lab interface yet. It defines what the next production rebuild must preserve, revise, and certify.

## 1. Course role

The Power and Flourishing Laboratory is the capstone after Chapter 12.

The historical chapters teach the Tool Rack. The chapter workbooks require focused use of one primary Tool under pressure. The Lab changes the task: students must decide which Tools actually belong in a complex system, in what order, what each contributes, what judgment is justified, what repair should be attempted, who may authorize it, and what would require reopening.

The governing movement remains:

**MAP → TRIAGE → SELECT → SEQUENCE → JUDGE → REPAIR → REOPEN**

A strong response does **not** use all twelve Tools. The canonical selection rule remains **3–5 primary Tools**.

## 2. Chapter 12 / PFLAB boundary

The final Chapter 12 workbook uses **Maathai / Green Belt Movement + Kijani** with the **Dependency / Effective-Capability Audit**.

The PFLAB should **not** simply continue the Maathai case.

The Lab remains a transfer environment built around the fictional **Pathway Support System / Student-Success-System** case. This forces students to carry the accumulated Tool Rack into a new institutional system rather than mistaking Chapter 12's Tool for the default capstone answer.

**Chapter 12 case mastery ≠ automatic PFLAB Tool selection.**

## 3. Power and Flourishing

Power and Flourishing remain organizing questions, not Tools 13 and 14.

**Power** asks who can shape conditions, options, representation, burdens, contestability, authorization, and reopening.

**Flourishing** asks what real forms of agency, security, relationship, learning, development, and opportunity affected people can actually exercise under those conditions.

These questions organize cross-tool inquiry. They do not substitute for the twelve historical Tools.

## 5. Ethics-first, FSP under the hood

The Lab remains an Intro Ethics capstone. Students should not be required to master FSP terminology as a separate framework.

Later FSP work should improve the governance architecture quietly:

- **Map ≠ World ≠ Person**
- **Generate ≠ Recommend ≠ Authorize**
- **Possibility ≠ recommendation ≠ authorization**
- **Current judgment ≠ final closure**
- explicit provenance
- explicit standing
- explicit claim ceilings
- explicit consequence tracking
- explicit Authorial Return
- explicit REOPEN

The student-facing language should remain ordinary ethical language unless an FSP distinction genuinely clarifies the task.

## 6. Lab sequence

### MAP

Recover the student's first representation of the system.

Record:
- what is directly known;
- what is inferred;
- whose perspectives are represented;
- who or what may be missing;
- institutions, rules, constraints, and relations;
- uncertainties that matter.

### TRIAGE

Identify the 3–5 ethical layers that are most decision-relevant before selecting Tools.

Triage is not Tool selection. It is the judgment about what kinds of ethical pressure the case contains.

### SELECT

Choose **3–5** primary Tools from the canonical twelve.

For each Tool, state:
- why it belongs;
- what it may reveal;
- what it cannot establish alone.

Carry-forward from SUPER may inform selection but may not choose for the student.

### SEQUENCE

Put the selected Tools in a justified order.

The sequence must explain why the output of one inquiry improves the map needed by the next.

A mere list of Tool names is insufficient.

### JUDGE

Produce a bounded ethical judgment that distinguishes:
- evidence;
- inference;
- uncertainty;
- recommendation;
- standing;
- authorization;
- claim ceiling.

The Lab must preserve:

**A good recommendation ≠ authority to impose it.**

### REPAIR

Design a repair aimed at the diagnosed mechanism.

The repair must state:
- what should change;
- who would implement it;
- who has standing to authorize it;
- who may contest or appeal;
- what burdens the repair shifts;
- which capabilities it expands or constrains;
- what evidence would show that it is working.

A repair is not merely a preferred outcome.

### REOPEN

State concrete conditions that should cause the judgment or repair to be reconsidered.

Examples include:
- failed assumptions;
- unexpected harm;
- changed institutional conditions;
- newly affected people;
- new evidence;
- unequal burden discovered after implementation;
- loss of standing or contestability.

REOPEN must be operational rather than ceremonial.

3. Power Map
4. Known Evidence
5. Provenance
6. Unknowns
7. Triage Layers
8. Selected Tools

10. Sequence Justification
11. Bounded Judgment
12. Standing and Authorization
13. Repair Proposal
14. Capability Effects
15. Burden-Shift Check
16. Claim Ceiling
17. REOPEN Conditions
18. Authorial Return

**Authorial Return** is the student's final governed return to the ethical problem: what they now judge, what remains uncertain, what they are prepared to recommend, what they are **not** authorized to decide, and what would cause them to reopen the judgment.

## 8. SUPER handoff rule

The existing `ETHICS-PFLAB-HANDOFF-1` contract remains the required entry path.

The handoff may provide:
- twelve Tool Records;
- twelve READY Output Cards;
- chapter claim ceilings;
- carry-forward questions;
- prior student-authored work.

The handoff may **not**:
- choose the student's 3–5 Tools;
- determine Tool order;
- attribute values or motives to the student;
- rank repairs;
- authorize the final judgment.

**Carry-forward ≠ automatic selection.**

## 9. Case design requirement

The Pathway Support System case remains fictional and institutionally complex.

The production case must include enough pressure to make several Tool families genuinely relevant, including:
- institutional purpose;
- prediction or classification uncertainty;
- unequal burdens;
- representation of affected people;
- background constraints;
- authority and appeal;
- welfare effects;
- effective capability;
- possible repairs with tradeoffs.

No single Tool should solve the case by itself.

## 10. Repair and capability reconciliation

Every repair proposal must include a **capability-effects check**.

The Lab must ask not only whether the intervention helps its intended target but whether it silently consumes another person's time, agency, care labor, opportunity, or standing.

Freeze the firewall:

**Capability expansion for one person ≠ justified capability contraction for another.**

Tradeoffs may sometimes be justified, but they must be made visible and defended.

## 11. Claim ceiling

The Lab may support a bounded recommendation and repair proposal.

It does not establish:
- that one ethical Tool is universally superior;
- that use of more Tools produces a better answer;
- that institutional effectiveness proves legitimacy;
- that predicted benefit authorizes coercion;
- that student analysis confers real institutional authority;
- that a fictional case establishes empirical claims about actual colleges.

PFLAB-2 and the planned SUPER redo should share the same canonical Tool names, claim-ceiling grammar, handoff schema, persistence assumptions, and launch ledger.

Neither build should silently redefine the other.

**PRESSBOOKS READY + SUPER READY + PFLAB READY = COURSE READY**

This specification is ready to govern a production rebuild when:

- its executable contract passes;
- the twelve Tool names match the final Pressbook;
- the Chapter 12/PFLAB case boundary is preserved;
- Power and Flourishing remain organizing questions;
- the 3–5 Tool rule remains locked;
- FSP remains governance infrastructure rather than a new student prerequisite;
- the final Lab Record includes bounded judgment, repair, authorization, capability effects, claim ceiling, REOPEN, and Authorial Return.
